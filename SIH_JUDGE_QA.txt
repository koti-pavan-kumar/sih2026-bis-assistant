SECTION 1: PROJECT OVERVIEW QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "Explain your project in 30 seconds."
A: "ManakMitra is an AI assistant that helps Indian businesses understand
    which government quality standards apply to their products. You ask a
    question in any of 22 Indian languages — Hindi, Telugu, Bengali, Tamil,
    whatever — and it gives you a complete answer: which standards apply,
    what tests to run, where to go test your product, whether it's
    mandatory, how to get certified, and what documents you need. All
    answers come from real government documents, not made-up AI text."

Q: "What problem does this solve?"
A: "India has 63 million small businesses (MSMEs) employing 110 million
    people. Most of them don't know which BIS quality standards apply to
    their products. The standards are in English, and 90% of these business
    owners speak only regional languages. So they either don't comply and
    face penalties, or they pay expensive consultants thousands of rupees.
    ManakMitra solves this by being a free, multilingual AI assistant that
    explains everything in their language with verified government sources."

Q: "Why should anyone use this instead of Google?"
A: "Google gives you 10 million search results, and you have to figure out
    which ones are correct. ManakMitra searches through actual indexed BIS
    standard documents and gives you a single, structured answer with exact
    IS numbers and clause references. It's like having a compliance expert
    who has read every standard and speaks your language."

Q: "What makes this different from ChatGPT?"
A: "Three things. First, ManakMitra uses RAG — it searches our database of
    real BIS standards before answering, so it cites specific IS numbers
    with clause references. ChatGPT makes things up (hallucination).
    Second, ManakMitra speaks 22 Indian languages with auto-detection.
    Third, it has specialized knowledge — BIS offices, certification
    processes, testing requirements — that ChatGPT doesn't have."

SECTION 2: TECHNICAL QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "Explain the technology stack."
A: "Frontend: React with Vite, Tailwind CSS, deployed on Vercel (free).
    Backend: Python FastAPI, deployed on Render (free).
    AI/ML: ONNX Runtime for embeddings (80MB, no PyTorch needed),
    FAISS for fast document search, Google Gemini for generating answers.
    Data: 28 indexed BIS standards in PDF format, 25+ BIS office locations,
    5 certification type guides. Everything runs for free."

Q: "What is RAG and why did you use it?"
A: "RAG stands for Retrieval Augmented Generation. Instead of asking the
    AI to answer from memory (which can be wrong), we first search our
    database of real BIS documents, find the relevant sections, and then
    give those sections to the AI along with the question. This way, the
    AI's answer is based on actual government documents, not imagination.
    It's the same technique used by Bloomberg, Google, and Microsoft for
    enterprise search."

Q: "Why ONNX instead of PyTorch?"
A: "PyTorch is 500MB and needs GPU support. ONNX Runtime is 80MB and runs
    on any CPU. Our embedding model (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) works identically
    in both — same 384-dimensional vectors, same accuracy. But ONNX lets
    us deploy on free hosting tiers with only 512MB RAM. Same quality,
    10x less memory, zero GPU needed."

Q: "How does FAISS work?"
A: "FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) is a library for finding
    similar items in large datasets. We convert each BIS standard into
    numerical vectors (embeddings). When a user asks a question, we
    convert the question to a vector too, then FAISS finds the 5 most
    similar document chunks in milliseconds — even with thousands of
    documents. It's like Google search but for meaning, not keywords."

Q: "How do you handle 22 languages?"
A: "The process has 4 steps: (1) Detect the input language using langdetect.
    (2) Translate the question to English using deep-translator (Google's
    translation API). (3) Run RAG search and generate the answer in
    English. (4) Translate the answer back to the user's original
    language. This happens transparently — the user just types in their
    language and gets a response in the same language."

Q: "What if Gemini API goes down?"
A: "We have a template fallback system. If the Gemini API fails or returns
    an error, the system extracts the relevant text from our documents
    and formats it into the 6-section response without the AI. It's not
    as polished, but users still get the information. We also support
    Ollama for fully offline operation."

Q: "How many standards do you have?"
A: "We currently have 28 indexed BIS standards covering electronics,
    construction, food safety, leather, steel, cement, electrical
    appliances, drinking water, LED lighting, and more. Our auto-fetch
    feature automatically adds new standards when BIS publishes them,
    so the knowledge base grows without manual work."

Q: "How does auto-fetch work?"
A: "We built a web scraper that checks bis.gov.in regularly. When it
    finds a new standard, it downloads the PDF, extracts the text,
    breaks it into chunks, converts each chunk to a vector, and adds
    it to our FAISS index. This means ManakMitra automatically knows
    about new standards without anyone manually updating anything."

Q: "What is the 6-section response format?"
A: "Every answer is structured into 6 sections:
    1. Applicable IS Standards — which standards apply and why
    2. Testing Requirements — what specific tests are needed
    3. Where to Test — nearest BIS offices with phone numbers
    4. Mandatory or Voluntary? — legal basis for compliance
    5. Certification Process — step-by-step guide with official links
    6. Documents Required — complete checklist of documents needed
    This format ensures users get actionable, complete answers."

Q: "How do you prevent AI hallucination?"
A: "Three mechanisms: (1) RAG limits responses to only what's in our
    indexed documents — the AI can't make up IS numbers. (2) Citation
    verification checks that every IS number mentioned actually exists
    in our index. (3) The structured 6-section format forces specific,
    verifiable output instead of free-form text. If the AI doesn't have
    enough information, it says so instead of guessing."

Q: "What is the difference between ONNX and PyTorch embeddings?"
A: "No difference in output. Both produce the same 384-dimensional
    vectors for the same input text. The only difference is runtime:
    PyTorch needs 500MB RAM and optional GPU. ONNX needs 80MB RAM and
    runs on CPU. For our use case (indexing ~28 documents), ONNX is
    more than sufficient and allows free hosting."

Q: "Why did you choose Gemini over OpenAI?"
A: "Gemini has a free tier with generous limits (60 requests/minute).
    OpenAI charges per token. For a free public service like ManakMitra,
    cost matters. Gemini 3.6 Flash is also very fast and handles
    structured prompts well. We built an abstraction layer so we can
    switch to any LLM (OpenAI, Ollama, Mistral) with a config change."

Q: "How does the office finder work?"
A: "We have data for 25+ BIS regional offices across India with their
    coordinates, phone numbers, services, and which products they test.
    When a user enables GPS, we calculate the distance from their
    location to each office using the Haversine formula and sort by
    nearest first. They can also filter by state or product type."

SECTION 3: SECURITY & PRIVACY QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "Where is user data stored?"
A: "Currently in the browser's localStorage. This is an MVP limitation.
    For production, we plan to use PostgreSQL with JWT-based
    authentication. All user data stays local to their browser — nothing
    is sent to our server except the chat query text and selected language."

Q: "Is the API key secure?"
A: "The Gemini API key is only in the backend .env file, never exposed
    to the frontend. All API calls go through our backend server, which
    acts as a proxy. The frontend never directly calls Gemini."

Q: "What about data privacy?"
A: "We follow a minimal data principle. Queries are processed in
    real-time and not stored on our server. Chat history stays in the
    browser. No personal information is sent to the LLM — only the
    query text and retrieved standard excerpts. No PII (personally
    identifiable information) is ever sent to external APIs."

Q: "Can someone abuse the API?"
A: "We're implementing rate limiting — 10 queries per minute per IP.
    For production, we'd add API keys, request logging, and abuse
    detection. The auto-fetch feature also has built-in rate limiting
    to avoid overwhelming the BIS website."

Q: "Is this production-ready?"
A: "It's an MVP (Minimum Viable Product) for SIH. The architecture
    is production-ready — RAG, ONNX, FAISS, FastAPI, React — these
    are enterprise-grade technologies. For full production, we'd add:
    PostgreSQL for user data, Redis caching, Kubernetes deployment,
    and comprehensive monitoring. But the core functionality works
    end-to-end and can serve real users today."

SECTION 5: TECHNICAL DEEP-DIVE QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "Walk me through what happens when someone asks a question."
A: "Step 1: User types 'What are the standards for cement?' in Hindi.
    Step 2: System detects Hindi, translates to English.
    Step 3: ONNX converts the question to a 384-dimension vector.
    Step 4: FAISS searches 115+ document chunks, finds top 5 matches.
    Step 5: System also adds BIS office data and certification guides.
    Step 6: Gemini receives the question + context, generates 6 sections.
    Step 7: Citation verification checks all IS numbers mentioned.
    Step 8: Response is translated back to Hindi.
    Step 9: Frontend renders 6 colored cards with the structured answer.
    Total time: 15-30 seconds."

Q: "How did you build the knowledge base?"
A: "We downloaded 28 BIS standard PDFs from bis.gov.in. Each PDF is
    parsed using PyPDF/PdfPlumber to extract text. The text is split
    into chunks of ~500 words with 50-word overlap (for context
    continuity). Each chunk is converted to a vector using ONNX
    embeddings. The vectors are stored in a FAISS index. The original
    text chunks are stored alongside for retrieval. Total: 115+
    indexed chunks from 28 standards."

Q: "What happens if the Gemini API quota is exceeded?"
A: "The system falls back to a template mode. It extracts the most
    relevant text chunks from our index and formats them into the
    6-section structure without AI generation. The response is less
    polished but still contains the actual standard information.
    We also log the failure and can switch to Ollama for offline
    operation."

Q: "Why did you split into 6 sections instead of one long answer?"
A: "Research shows that structured information is 40% more memorable
    than unstructured text. Each section answers a specific question
    the user has: What standards? How to test? Where to go? Is it
    required? How to certify? What documents? This eliminates the
    need to read through paragraphs of text. The color coding helps
    users quickly navigate to the section they care about."

Q: "How accurate are the IS number citations?"
A: "Every IS number in our response comes from one of 28 indexed
    standards. The RAG system ensures the AI only references standards
    that were actually retrieved. Citation verification further checks
    that mentioned IS numbers exist in our index. If the AI mentions
    an IS number not in our index, it's flagged and removed. This gives
    us near-100% citation accuracy for indexed standards."

Q: "What is the chunking strategy?"
A: "Each PDF is parsed page by page. Text is split into chunks of
    approximately 500 words with 50-word overlap between consecutive
    chunks. This overlap ensures that information at chunk boundaries
    isn't lost. Each chunk is labeled with its source IS number, page
    number, and section. This metadata is used for citation
    verification and source linking in the response."

SECTION 6: DEPLOYMENT & COST QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "Where is it hosted?"
A: "Frontend on Vercel (free tier, global CDN, auto-deploy on git push).
    Backend on Render (free tier, 512MB RAM, auto-deploy on git push).
    Both deploy automatically when we push code to GitHub. Total hosting
    cost: ₹0."

Q: "What are the limitations of free hosting?"
A: "Render free tier has 512MB RAM and spins down after 15 minutes of
    inactivity (first request takes ~30 seconds to wake up). Vercel free
    tier has generous limits for frontend hosting. For production, we'd
    upgrade to Render's Starter plan ($7/month) or deploy on Oracle Cloud
    Always Free tier (24GB RAM, never spins down)."

Q: "What is the ongoing cost?"
A: "Hosting: ₹0 (free tiers). Gemini API: ₹0 (free tier, 60 req/min).
    Translation: ₹0 (Google Translate free tier). Total ongoing cost
    for an MVP: ₹0/month. For production at scale, we estimate
    ₹5,000-10,000/month for paid hosting and API costs."

Q: "How does auto-deploy work?"
A: "We push code to GitHub's main branch. Vercel detects the change and
    rebuilds the React frontend (~30 seconds). Render detects the change
    and rebuilds the Python backend (~3-5 minutes, including ML model
    download). Both are live within 5 minutes of a git push. No manual
    deployment needed."

Q: "Can this be deployed on-premise for government?"
A: "Yes. The entire stack can run on a single government server.
    Replace Gemini with Ollama for offline LLM. The ONNX model and
    FAISS index run locally. FastAPI serves both the API and can serve
    the React build as static files. One server, zero external
    dependencies, full data sovereignty."

SECTION 8: SCALABILITY & FUTURE QUESTIONS
================================================================================

Q: "What is your roadmap?"
A: "Phase 1 (Current): 28 standards, 22 languages, 6-section format.
    Phase 2 (3 months): 500+ standards, PostgreSQL, mobile app.
    Phase 3 (6 months): Document upload & analysis, compliance tracking,
    AI-powered gap analysis. Phase 4 (12 months): All 28,000+ standards,
    BIS official partnership, field inspector app, API for third parties."

Q: "How do you plan to index all 28,000+ standards?"
A: "Our auto-fetch pipeline is designed for this. It scrapes bis.gov.in,
    downloads PDFs, extracts text, chunks content, and indexes
    automatically. To scale to 28,000 standards, we'd run the pipeline
    as a background job on a schedule (daily). The FAISS index can handle
    millions of vectors efficiently. The bottleneck is PDF parsing, which
    we can parallelize."

Q: "Can this be extended beyond BIS?"
A: "Yes. The RAG architecture is domain-agnostic. The same system can be
    built for: (1) Bureau of Indian Standards → BIS, (2) FSSAI → Food
    safety standards, (3) CPCB → Environmental compliance, (4) BEE →
    Energy efficiency standards, (5) Legal metrology → Weights & measures.
    Swap the document corpus, and the system works for any regulatory body."

Q: "What about mobile deployment?"
A: "The frontend is already responsive (works on mobile browsers). For a
    native experience, we can build a React Native app that wraps the
    same APIs. The ONNX model runs on-device for offline embedding
    generation, and FAISS works on mobile too. This would be ideal for
    field inspectors visiting factory floors."

Q: "How do you handle different product categories?"
A: "BIS organizes standards by product categories (electronics,
    construction, food, etc.). Our system indexes standards by category
    and tags each chunk with its category. When a user asks about a
    specific product, we filter by category first, then do semantic
    search within that category for more precise results."
