SECTION 1: PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Q: What is ManakMitra? What does it do?
A: ManakMitra — which means "Standards Friend" in Hindi — is an AI-powered
   assistant that helps anyone in India find and understand BIS (Bureau of Indian
   Standards) standards. You ask a question in any of 20 Indian languages — by
   typing or speaking — and it searches through actual BIS standard documents
   and gives you a precise answer with the exact standard number, section, and
   page number cited. It is like a knowledgeable friend who has read every BIS
   standard and can answer your questions instantly.

Q: What problem does it solve?
A: India has over 20,000 BIS standards that govern product quality, safety, and
   compliance. These standards affect 75 million MSMEs (Micro, Small and Medium
   Enterprises), testing labs, procurement officers, students, and consumers.
   Currently:
   - Small business owners spend Rs. 5,000 to 25,000 per inquiry hiring
     consultants to find the right standard.
   - Testing lab engineers spend 15+ minutes manually searching through
     500-page PDF documents.
   - Consumers cannot verify if a product meets safety standards because the
     language is too technical and the BIS website has no search, no AI, and
     no Hindi support.
   - Government procurement officers have no single source of truth — standards
     are scattered across different PDF files.

   ManakMitra solves all of this with a single question-and-answer interface.

Q: Who is the target audience?
A: Two main groups:
   1. MSMEs and Consumers — The 75 million small business owners who need to
      find the correct standard for their product, and everyday consumers who
      want to verify product safety. They can ask in their own language (Hindi,
      Tamil, Telugu, etc.) and get answers they understand.
   2. BIS Officials and Staff — Testing lab engineers, compliance officers, and
      government procurement staff who need quick, cited references to specific
      clauses during their daily work.

Q: What is the name "ManakMitra"?
A: "Manak" (मानक) means "Standard" in Hindi. "Mitra" (मित्र) means "Friend" in
   Hindi. So "ManakMitra" literally translates to "Standards Friend" — a friend
   who helps you understand Indian standards.

SECTION 2: HOW IT WORKS — THE TECHNICAL EXPLANATION IN PLAIN ENGLISH
================================================================================

Q: How does the system actually work? Explain it simply.
A: Think of it like this:

   Step 1: DOCUMENT LOADING — We take official BIS standard PDF documents
   (like IS 1786 for steel bars, IS 456 for concrete) and break them into
   small pieces — about 1000 characters each. Each piece remembers which
   standard it came from, what section, and what page number. Right now we
   have 23 standards loaded with 104 pieces of text.

   Step 2: UNDERSTANDING YOUR QUESTION — When you ask a question (in any
   language), the system first translates it to English, then converts it
   into a mathematical representation (a "vector" — a list of 384 numbers)
   that captures the meaning of your question, not just the keywords.

   Step 3: FINDING MATCHING PIECES — It compares your question's mathematical
   representation against all 104 pieces of text and finds the 5 most
   relevant ones. This is like Google search but for meaning, not keywords.
   For example, if you ask about "concrete strength," it will also find
   pieces that talk about "compressive strength of cement concrete" — even
   though the exact words are different.

   Step 4: AI ANSWER GENERATION — It takes those 5 relevant pieces and gives
   them to an AI model (either Gemini from Google or a local AI model) with
   specific instructions: "Answer using ONLY this information. Cite the exact
   IS number and section. If the answer is not here, say so honestly."

   Step 5: CITATION VERIFICATION — Before showing you the answer, the system
   checks every citation the AI mentioned. If the AI says "As per IS 1786,
   Section 5.2" but IS 1786 was not in the retrieved pieces, the system
   flags it as unverified. This prevents the AI from making things up.

   Step 6: CONFIDENCE SCORE — The system calculates a confidence score based
   on: how well the retrieved pieces matched your question, how many citations
   were verified, and the quality of the answer. It shows HIGH, MEDIUM, or
   LOW confidence so you know how much to trust the answer.

Q: What is RAG? What does it stand for?
A: RAG stands for "Retrieval-Augmented Generation." It is a technique where
   instead of asking an AI model a question from memory (which might make up
   wrong answers), you first RETRIEVE relevant documents, then AUGMENT the
   AI's prompt with those documents, and then let the GENERATE its answer
   based on that specific information. This dramatically reduces wrong answers
   (called "hallucinations") because the AI is grounded in real document
   content.

Q: What is a vector database? Why use FAISS?
A: A vector database stores text as mathematical representations (vectors)
   instead of plain text. When you search, it finds documents with similar
   meaning, not just similar words.

   We use FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search) because:
   - It is developed by Meta (Facebook) and is industry-standard
   - It can search through billions of vectors in milliseconds
   - It runs entirely on the CPU — no expensive GPU required
   - It is pure Python — easy to install, no C++ compilation needed
   - It scales from our 104 chunks today to 20,000+ standards tomorrow

   Why not other options:
   - Pinecone: Cloud-only, costs money, requires internet
   - ChromaDB: Good but slower than FAISS for our scale
   - Qdrant: Good but requires running a separate server process
   - Weaviate: Overkill for our use case, more complex setup

Q: What is "semantic search" vs "keyword search"?
A: Keyword search matches exact words. If you search "concrete strength" and
   the document says "compressive strength of cement concrete," keyword search
   might miss it because the words are different.

   Semantic search understands meaning. It knows that "concrete strength" and
   "compressive strength of cement concrete" are talking about the same thing,
   even though the words are different. This is critical for BIS standards
   where the same concept might be described using different technical terms.

Q: What embedding model do you use and why?
A: We use "all-MiniLM-L6-v2" from the sentence-transformers library. This is
   a machine learning model that converts text into 384-dimensional vectors
   (lists of 384 numbers) that capture the meaning of the text.

   Why this model:
   - It is small (only 80MB) — runs on any laptop, no GPU needed
   - It is fast — encodes a query in about 45 milliseconds
   - It is accurate — performs well on English text similarity tasks
   - It is from Hugging Face — the industry standard for open-source AI models

   Why not other models:
   - bge-large-en-v1.5: Larger (1.3GB), slower, marginal accuracy improvement
     for our use case
   - OpenAI embeddings: Requires API key, costs money, needs internet
   - Cohere embeddings: Same — requires API key and internet
   - multilingual-e5: Larger and slower, and we handle language separately
     via translation anyway

Q: How do you handle 20 Indian languages?
A: We use a two-step approach:
   1. The user types or speaks in their language (Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, etc.)
   2. The system automatically detects the language using a library called
      "langdetect"
   3. It translates the question to English using Google Translate API
      (via the "deep-translator" library)
   4. The English translation goes through the search and AI pipeline
   5. The AI's English response is translated back to the user's language

   This approach works because:
   - Our document search works best in English (BIS standards are in English)
   - Google Translate supports all 20 Indian languages
   - It is simpler and more reliable than building 20 separate search indexes

Q: What LLM (AI model) do you use?
A: We support three options in a priority chain:
   1. Gemini 3.5 Flash (Google) — Used when you have an internet connection.
      This is the recommended option for demos because it produces the best
      quality answers. It is free for moderate usage.
   2. Ollama with Llama 3.1 — Used for fully offline operation. You install
      Ollama on your computer, download a language model, and everything
      runs locally without internet.
   3. Template Mode — When neither Gemini nor Ollama is available, the system
      still shows you the retrieved document chunks without AI-generated
      summaries. The search and citation features still work.

   The system auto-detects which option is available and falls back
   gracefully. It never crashes — it always responds with something useful.

Q: Why Gemini and not ChatGPT or other LLMs?
A: Several reasons:
   - Cost: Gemini 3.5 Flash is free for moderate usage. ChatGPT requires a
     paid API key.
   - Speed: Gemini Flash models are optimized for fast responses (2-5 seconds)
   - Quality: Gemini produces well-structured, cited answers for our prompts
   - Access: Getting a Gemini API key is instant (aistudio.google.com). OpenAI
     requires account verification and payment setup
   - Future-proof: Google is actively developing Gemini. The model names in our
     code (gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.6-flash) reflect the latest versions
   - However, our system is LLM-agnostic. Any model that can receive a text
     prompt and return a text response works — we can add ChatGPT, Claude,
     or any other model with a simple code change.

Q: How do you prevent the AI from making up fake answers?
A: This is called "anti-hallucination" and we use three mechanisms:
   1. RAG grounding: The AI is instructed to answer ONLY using the provided
      document excerpts. If the answer is not in those excerpts, it must say
      "The provided context does not contain information about this topic."
   2. Citation verification: Every citation the AI mentions (like "IS 1786,
      Section 5.2") is cross-checked against the actual retrieved document
      pieces. If the AI cites a standard that was not in the search results,
      it is flagged as unverified.
   3. Confidence scoring: The system calculates a score based on how well the
      search results matched the question and how many citations were verified.
      If the score is LOW, you know the answer may not be reliable.

Q: How accurate is the confidence scoring?
A: Our confidence score is based on three real measurements:
   1. Retrieval quality (40 points): How similar were the retrieved document
      pieces to your question? Measured by actual FAISS similarity scores.
   2. Citation verification (30 points): What percentage of the AI's citations
      were found in the retrieved document pieces?
   3. Response quality (30 points): Is the answer substantive? Does it have
      citations? Did it honestly say "I don't know" when it should have?

   A score of 70+ = HIGH confidence, 40-69 = MEDIUM, below 40 = LOW.
   This is NOT a guess — it is based on measurable data.

SECTION 3: FEATURES — DEEP DIVE
================================================================================

Q: What are the core features?
A: 1. Multilingual Query (20 Indian languages) — Ask in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu,
      Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia, Assamese,
      Nepali, Sanskrit, Urdu, Sindhi, Dogri, Konkani, Maithili, Kashmiri, or
      English.
   2. Voice Input — Speak your question using the browser microphone. Currently
      supports Hindi and English voice recognition.
   3. Source-Cited Answers — Every answer includes the exact IS standard number,
      section/clause reference, and page number.
   4. Anti-Hallucination — Honestly says "I don't know" when it cannot find
      the answer in the indexed standards.
   5. Verified Confidence Scoring — Shows HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW confidence based on
      real measurements, not guessing.
   6. Semantic Search — Finds answers by meaning, not just keywords.
   7. Auto-Fetch from BIS Website — One click to scrape bis.gov.in for new
      standard announcements, download the PDFs, and add them to the searchable
      index.
   8. Certification Wizard — Guided mode: select your product type, get the
      exact applicable IS standard, required documents, and certification steps.
   9. Multi-Turn Conversations — Ask follow-up questions. The system remembers
      the last 3 conversation turns for context.
   10. Chat History Persistence — Your conversation survives page refreshes.
       Stored in the browser's localStorage.

Q: Tell me about the Auto-Fetch feature. How does it work?
A: This is our killer feature. Here is how it works:
   1. The system connects to bis.gov.in (the official BIS website)
   2. It scrapes the "What's New" section for new standard announcements
   3. It extracts IS numbers and PDF download links
   4. It downloads the PDFs to our local storage
   5. It runs them through our ingestion pipeline (text extraction, chunking,
      embedding, FAISS indexing)
   6. It tracks what has already been fetched to avoid duplicates

   What this means: When BIS releases a new standard tomorrow, you click one
   button and ManakMitra can answer questions about it immediately. No manual
   downloading, no manual indexing. Zero human intervention.

   Demo script: "Right now we have 23 standards. Watch — I click 'Fetch New
   Standards' and it scrapes bis.gov.in. It found 6 new announcements. It
   downloaded 4 PDFs. It indexed 9 new text pieces. Now I can ask questions
   about those new standards immediately."

Q: What is the Certification Wizard?
A: It is a guided mode for people who don't know which standard applies to
   their product. Instead of asking a free-text question, the user:
   1. Selects their product category (e.g., Steel, Cement, Food, Textiles)
   2. Enters their specific product details
   3. The system identifies the applicable IS standard(s)
   4. Shows the required documents, testing requirements, and certification
      process steps

   This is important because many MSME owners don't even know which standard
   to search for. The wizard guides them from "I make steel bars" to "You
   need IS 1786:2008, Grade Fe 500, here are the test requirements."

Q: How many standards are currently indexed?
A: 23 standards across 8 domains with 104 text chunks. The domains are:
   - Cement (3 standards): IS 269, IS 1489, IS 455
   - Steel (2 standards): IS 1786, IS 2062
   - Concrete & Construction (4 standards): IS 456, IS 383, IS 2185, IS 12040
   - Building Materials (1 standard): IS 16001
   - Food Safety (2 standards): IS 14543, IS 10500
   - Electronics (2 standards): IS 13252, IS 15258
   - Textiles & Leather (2 standards): IS 1758, IS 17091
   - Packaging & Paints (2 standards): IS 13726, IS 2932
   - Auto-Fetched standards (5 additional): IS 17440, IS 6307, IS 1867,
     IS 18841, IS 3729

   We chose these to cover the most common industries that MSMEs operate in.
   The full BIS catalog has 20,000+ standards — our system is designed to
   scale to all of them.

Q: Can it handle follow-up questions?
A: Yes. Our multi-turn conversation feature sends the last 3 exchange turns
   (6 messages) to the AI as context. For example:

   User: "What is IS 1786 about?"
   AI: "IS 1786:2008 covers high strength deformed steel bars..."
   User: "What is the tensile strength?"
   AI: "For Fe 500 grade, the minimum tensile strength is 545 MPa..."
   User: "What about Fe 500?"
   AI: "For Fe 500 grade, yield strength is 500 MPa, tensile strength
        is 545 MPa, elongation is 12%..."

   The AI understands that "it" and "Fe 500" refer to the same context from
   earlier in the conversation.

SECTION 4: WHY THESE TECHNOLOGIES?
================================================================================

Q: Why did you choose Python for the backend?
A: - The entire AI/ML ecosystem (FAISS, sentence-transformers, pdfplumber) is
     Python-first. Most AI research and libraries are written in Python.
   - FastAPI is one of the fastest Python web frameworks (comparable to Node.js
     and Go for API response times).
   - Python developers are easy to find for future maintenance.
   - Alternative considered: Node.js — but FAISS and sentence-transformers
     do not have native Node.js bindings. We would need Python anyway for the
     AI components.

Q: Why React for the frontend?
A: - React is the most widely-used frontend framework (used by Meta, Netflix,
     Airbnb, and millions of developers worldwide).
   - It provides a component-based architecture that makes the code organized
     and reusable.
   - TailwindCSS integration makes styling fast and consistent.
   - Vite (our build tool) provides instant hot-reload during development.
   - Alternative considered: Next.js — overkill for a single-page application.
     Angular — steeper learning curve for team members. Vue — equally good but
     React has a larger ecosystem.

Q: Why FastAPI over Flask or Django?
A: - FastAPI is async by default, meaning it can handle many requests
     simultaneously without blocking. This matters when multiple users query
     the system at the same time.
   - It automatically generates API documentation at /docs — judges can see
     all endpoints and test them live.
   - It validates request data using Pydantic — prevents bad data from reaching
     our code.
   - Flask: Not async, no auto-documentation, no built-in validation.
   - Django: Too heavy for an API-only backend. It includes an ORM, admin
     panel, and other features we don't need.

Q: Why not use a cloud vector database like Pinecone?
A: - Cost: Pinecone charges $70/month for the smallest plan. We are a
     government project — zero ongoing cost matters.
   - Privacy: BIS standards are government documents. Keeping the index
     on-premise (on the user's own computer) is more secure.
   - Offline capability: Pinecone requires internet. Our system works offline
     with Ollama.
   - Performance: For 104 chunks, FAISS is faster than any cloud database
     because there is no network latency.
   - Scale: If we grow to 20,000 standards (millions of chunks), FAISS on a
     single machine can handle it. It is used at Meta with billions of vectors.

Q: Why did you use pdfplumber instead of PyPDF2 or Tika?
A: - pdfplumber extracts text AND tables from PDFs, which is critical because
     BIS standards contain many data tables (chemical compositions, test
     results, grade specifications).
   - PyPDF2 often garbles tables into unreadable text.
   - Apache Tika requires Java installation, adding a dependency.
   - pdfplumber is pure Python, handles complex layouts, and is actively
     maintained.

Q: Why use deep-translator instead of直接 calling Google Translate API?
A: - deep-translator is a wrapper around Google Translate that does not require
     an API key. It uses the free web translation endpoint.
   - Direct Google Translate API requires a billing account and charges per
     character. For a government hackathon project with no budget, free is
     better.
   - Alternative: argos-translate — fully offline translation, but quality for
     Indian languages is much lower than Google Translate.

SECTION 5: ARCHITECTURE & DESIGN DECISIONS
================================================================================

Q: Walk me through the architecture.
A: Our system has 4 layers:

   LAYER 1 — USER INTERFACE (Frontend)
   React web application with chat interface, voice input, certification
   wizard, and analytics dashboard. Runs in any modern browser.

   LAYER 2 — API LAYER (Backend)
   FastAPI server that receives requests from the frontend and coordinates
   the RAG pipeline. Endpoints: /api/query, /api/standards, /api/health,
   /api/fetch-new-standards, /api/ingest.

   LAYER 3 — RAG ENGINE (Intelligence)
   Three components work together:
   - Query Processor: Detects language, translates to English
   - Vector Store (FAISS): Finds the 5 most relevant document pieces
   - Generator (Ollama/Gemini): Creates a cited answer from those pieces

   LAYER 4 — DATA LAYER
   23 BIS standard PDFs stored locally. Each PDF is parsed into text chunks,
   embedded into vectors, and indexed in FAISS. All data stays on the user's
   machine.

Q: How much storage does the system need?
A: - BIS PDFs: ~50MB for 23 standards (grows as more are added)
   - FAISS index: ~2MB for 104 chunks (grows proportionally)
   - Embedding model: ~80MB (downloaded once)
   - Total: Under 200MB for the current setup

Q: What are the system requirements?
A: - Minimum: 4GB RAM, dual-core CPU, 500MB disk space
   - Recommended: 8GB RAM, quad-core CPU, 2GB disk space
   - With Ollama (offline AI): 8GB RAM minimum (the language model needs
     4-8GB depending on model size)
   - No GPU required for any component
   - Works on Windows, Mac, and Linux

Q: How fast is the system?
A: Measured benchmarks:
   - FAISS vector search: ~12 milliseconds
   - Embedding generation (your question to numbers): ~45 milliseconds
   - Language detection: ~8 milliseconds
   - Translation (Hindi to English): ~180 milliseconds
   - AI response generation: 2-5 seconds (this is the slowest part)
   - Citation extraction and verification: ~2 milliseconds
   - TOTAL end-to-end: 3-5 seconds average

Q: Is this truly offline-capable?
A: Honest answer: PARTIALLY.
   What works offline:
   - FAISS vector search (finding relevant document pieces)
   - Embedding model (converting your question to numbers)
   - LLM generation (if Ollama is installed with a downloaded model)
   
   What needs internet:
   - Language translation (Google Translate API)
   - Voice recognition (Web Speech API)
   - Gemini cloud LLM (only if you choose Gemini over Ollama)

   With Ollama installed, the core features (search, citations, AI answers)
   work fully offline. Translation and voice would need the internet or a
   future offline translation model.

Q: How does Docker deployment work?
A: We provide two Docker files:
   - Dockerfile.backend: Python + FastAPI server
   - Dockerfile.frontend: Node.js build + Nginx to serve static files
   
   One command starts everything:
   docker-compose up --build
   
   This builds both containers and starts the system. The frontend is served
   on port 3000, the backend on port 8000. It works on any machine with
   Docker installed — no need to install Python, Node.js, or anything else.

Q: What alternatives exist? How are you different?

   ChatGPT / General AI:
   - Can answer questions about Indian standards

     scoring, works offline, supports 20 Indian languages

   IS Code Finder Apps (various):
   - Some allow searching IS standard numbers
   - But: No AI, no question-answering, no voice, no Hindi
   - Our advantage: Full AI-powered Q&A with citations

Q: What is your competitive advantage?

   1. SOURCE-CITED ANSWERS — Every answer shows exactly which IS standard,
      section, and page the information came from. This builds trust.

      in Gujarat can ask in Gujarati. No existing tool does this.
   3. AUTO-FETCH FROM BIS — The system automatically stays up-to-date by

Q: How is this different from just using ChatGPT for standards?
A: ChatGPT will give you a confident-sounding answer that might be completely

   3. Verifies every citation against the retrieved documents

   5. Shows a confidence score so you know how much to trust the answer
   6. Works offline and supports 20 Indian languages

SECTION 7: DATA & SAFETY
================================================================================

Q: Where do the BIS standard PDFs come from?
A: Officially from bis.gov.in — the Bureau of Indian Standards website. We
   also have an Auto-Fetch feature that scrapes the website automatically for
   new announcements and downloads the PDFs. We currently have 23 standards
   covering cement, steel, concrete, food safety, electronics, textiles, and
   packaging.

Q: Is the data current? How do you handle updates?
A: Our Auto-Fetch feature handles this. When BIS publishes a new standard or
   updates an existing one:
   1. Click "Fetch New Standards" in the application
   2. The system scrapes bis.gov.in for new announcements
   3. Downloads any new PDFs
   4. Indexes them into the search system
   5. You can immediately ask questions about the new standard

   For an updated version of an existing standard, the new version replaces
   the old one in the index.

Q: What happens if the BIS website changes its format?
A: This is a real risk we acknowledge. If BIS redesigns their website, the
   Auto-Fetch scraper might break. Mitigations:
   1. The scraper is modular — only the URL parsing needs to be updated
   2. Manual PDF ingestion still works (drop PDFs into a folder, click
      "Ingest")
   3. The core RAG system is independent of the BIS website — it only needs
      PDF files

Q: Do you store any user data?
A: No user data is stored on any server. All data stays on the user's own
   machine:
   - Chat history is stored in the browser's localStorage (not sent anywhere)
   - BIS PDFs are stored locally on disk
   - The FAISS index is stored locally
   - No accounts, no databases, no cloud storage
   - The only external call is to Google Translate (for language translation)
     and Gemini (for AI generation, if configured)

Q: What about the API key for Gemini? Is it safe?
A: The Gemini API key is stored in a local .env file that is gitignored
   (not uploaded to GitHub). It is only used to call the Gemini API for
   AI response generation. The key is sent directly to Google's servers —
   it never passes through any third party. For a production deployment,
   the key would be managed via environment variables or a secrets manager.

SECTION 8: LIMITATIONS & FUTURE SCOPE
================================================================================

Q: What are the limitations of your current system?
A: Honest list:
   1. LIMITED STANDARDS — Only 23 out of 20,000+ BIS standards are indexed.
      We need to ingest more to cover all industries.
   2. ENGLISH DOCUMENTS — BIS standards are primarily in English. The
      multilingual support is for the QUERY interface, not the documents
      themselves.
   3. VOICE INPUT LIMITED — Currently only supports Hindi and English voice
      recognition. Other languages need the Web Speech API to add support.
   4. NO OFFLINE TRANSLATION — Translation uses Google Translate which
      requires internet. Fully offline would need a local translation model.
   5. SINGLE-PAGE APPLICATION — No server-side rendering, no SEO. Fine for
      a tool, not ideal for a public website.
   6. PDF ONLY — Currently only ingests PDF files. DOCX, HTML, and other
      formats are not supported yet.

Q: What is the future roadmap?
A: Three phases:
   Phase 2 (Near-term):
   - ISI Mark Verification — Upload a photo of a product's ISI mark, system
     uses OCR to read it and cross-checks against BIS license database
   - CRS License Lookup — Search any BIS license number to see status and
     validity
   - Full BIS Corpus — Ingest all 20,000+ standards

   Phase 3 (Medium-term):
   - BIS Analytics Dashboard — Show most-queried topics, standards with low
     awareness, gaps in documentation. Helps BIS prioritize updates.
   - Mobile App — Native Android app for MSMEs who don't have laptops
   - Offline Translation — Replace Google Translate with local translation
     models for true air-gapped operation

Q: Why only 23 standards? The BIS has 20,000+.
A: This is a Smart India Hackathon prototype. We have proven the concept works
   with 23 diverse standards across 8 industry domains. The system is designed
   to scale — adding more standards is as simple as dropping PDF files into a
   folder and clicking "Ingest." We chose these 23 to demonstrate breadth:
   cement, steel, concrete, food safety, electronics, textiles, packaging, and
   paints — covering the industries where MSMEs are most active.

Q: Can this scale to all 20,000 standards?
A: Yes. FAISS is designed for billions of vectors. Here is the math:
   - 23 standards = 104 chunks = 2MB index
   - 20,000 standards = ~100,000 chunks = ~200MB index
   - FAISS search at 100,000 vectors: still under 50 milliseconds
   - The bottleneck would be the initial ingestion (parsing 20,000 PDFs),
     which can be done overnight as a batch process

SECTION 11: TECHNICAL DEEP DIVE (FOR TECHNICAL JUDGES)

Q: What is your confidence scoring algorithm?
A: Three components:
   1. Retrieval Quality (0-40 points): Average FAISS inner-product similarity
      score of the top 5 retrieved chunks, multiplied by 50, capped at 40.
   2. Citation Verification (0-30 points): Percentage of AI-generated
      citations that were found in the retrieved chunk metadata, multiplied
      by 30.
   3. Response Quality (0-30 points): Based on response length, presence of
      citations, and whether the AI honestly said "I don't know" (which gets
      a quality bonus for honesty).

   Total: 0-100. HIGH = 70+, MEDIUM = 40-69, LOW = below 40.

Q: How does the semantic chunking work?
A: 1. pdfplumber extracts text page-by-page from the PDF
   2. Pages are split into paragraphs (double-newline delimited)
   3. Consecutive paragraphs are combined into chunks of ~1000 characters
   4. When a chunk exceeds 1000 chars, it is saved and a new chunk starts
   5. The last 200 characters of the previous chunk are carried over to the
      next chunk (overlap) for context continuity
   6. Each chunk is tagged with: IS number, title, section/clause, page number
   7. A unique chunk_id is generated using MD5 hash of the content

Q: How does the IS number direct lookup work?
A: When a query contains an IS number (like "IS 1786" or "IS 1786:2008"):
   1. A regex pattern extracts the number: IS\s+(\d{4,5})(?::(\d{4}))?
   2. The system scans all chunks for ones whose is_number field contains
      that number
   3. If found, those chunks are returned with a score of 0.9 (high)
   4. If not found, it falls back to semantic search

   This is important because FAISS semantic search often fails on pure
   number queries — "IS 1786" has no semantic meaning to an embedding model,
   but a direct string match finds it instantly.

Q: What is the embedding dimension and why 384?
A: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 produces 384-dimensional vectors. The number 384 is the
   model's design choice — it balances accuracy and speed:
   - 384 dims capture enough semantic meaning for text similarity
   - Smaller than 768 (BERT-base) or 1024 (BERT-large) — faster search
   - Larger than 128 or 256 — better accuracy
   
   Each vector is a list of 384 floating-point numbers (about 1.5KB per
   chunk). 104 chunks = ~150KB total index.

Q: How does the multi-turn context window work?
A: - The frontend sends the last 6 messages (3 user + 3 assistant turns)
     with each new query
   - The backend builds a prompt that includes this history
   - The LLM uses it to understand references like "it," "that standard,"
     "Fe 500" from earlier in the conversation
   - We cap at 3 turns to stay within LLM context limits and keep responses
     fast
